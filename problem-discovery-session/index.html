The common threadAssumption audit

An idea is a bundle of assumptions.

  • A specific customer has this problem.
  • It occurs often enough to matter.
  • Existing alternatives are inadequate.
  • The customer wants change.
  • We can create a better outcome.
  • Someone will pay for that outcome.
Discovery turns assumptions into evidence.07
Discovery does not eliminate uncertainty. It reduces uncertainty enough to support the next decision.

Latent demandHidden ≠ imaginary

Demand may whisper through workarounds.

But founder conviction is not evidence.

  • The customer accepts the problem as normal.
  • Several partial solutions are combined.
  • Substantial manual effort is spent.
  • The consequence is paid for indirectly.
  • The task is abandoned.
  • Existing options are inaccessible.
What behaviour exists even when explicit demand does not?19
Absence of purchase is not proof of absence of need. But absence of behaviour should make us cautious. Seek effort, sacrifice, repeated frustration, indirect spend, or abandonment.
Activity / 7 minutesEvidence court

Rank the
evidence.

Strongest → weakest
  1. Eight customers said they loved the idea.
  2. Three retailers copy WhatsApp orders into notebooks.
  3. Twenty people joined a free waitlist.
  4. Four customers paid a refundable ₹500 deposit.

What does each prove—and not prove?20
Likely ranking: repeated use, deposit, observed workaround, waitlist, positive opinion. Context matters. A deposit shows commitment but not retention; repeated use shows value but may not show willingness to pay.

SynthesisProblem statement

Write what you know.
Label what you believe.

P6 / ProbeDesign the learning

Test the assumption most likely to kill the idea.

  1. What must be true?
  2. Which belief is both critical and uncertain?
  3. What behaviour would support it?
  4. What is the cheapest credible test?
  5. What result would change our decision?
The goal is learning—not defending the idea.25
Prioritise assumptions by impact and lack of evidence. Testing something easy but non-critical creates activity, not risk reduction.
MVPSmallest credible experiment

An MVP is not a small product. It is a small, credible test.

Need to learnPossible probe
Does the problem occur?Interview • observation • diary study
Will customers act?Qualified sign-up • booking
Will customers trust us?Concierge service • manual fulfilment
Can the approach work?Prototype • Wizard-of-Oz test
Will customers pay?Deposit • preorder • paid pilot
Will customers return?Limited live service
Build only what the learning requires.26
An early MVP can be manual and operationally inefficient. It is not designed to scale; it is designed to produce credible evidence.
Experiment canvasDefine before you run

Design the experiment on one page.

1 / Critical assumption

What must be true for the idea to work?

2 / Hypothesis

We believe [person] will [behaviour] because [problem].

3 / Participants

Who qualifies—and who must be excluded?

4 / Test + artefact

What will they encounter or do?

5 / Evidence + threshold

What observable result counts by when?

6 / Decision rule

If met: continue. If mixed: revise. If missed: stop or reframe.

Write the threshold before seeing results.27
Use this as a printable canvas. A strong threshold is observable and decision-linked—for example, five of ten qualified users complete a real booking within seven days. “Positive feedback” is not a threshold.

Turn the validated need into an open challenge.

What must improve for the customer?

Time, access, trust, cost, policy, capability.

4 / Solution routes

Generate at least five meaningfully different approaches.

Desirability × feasibility × viability × evidence.

Solutioning prompt: “Using this evidence-backed problem statement: [paste], generate 10 fundamentally different solution approaches. Respect these constraints: [paste]. Do not add features yet. Group ideas by mechanism, identify the assumption behind each, then recommend three concepts to prototype and explain what each prototype must teach us.”
Generate alternatives before selecting features.29
Use this only after a team has an evidence-backed problem. The prompt uses AI as a divergence partner, not as a substitute for customer evidence or a final decision-maker.
